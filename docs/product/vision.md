# Product Vision

## Vision

TrackSea is a personal marine journal that powers open marine science.

It helps people who spend time around the sea record what they encounter, learn what they have seen, and contribute trustworthy biodiversity observations that may support research and conservation.

## Who TrackSea serves

TrackSea is designed for people such as:

- swimmers;
- snorkellers;
- divers;
- surfers;
- fishers;
- sailors;
- marine photographers;
- naturalists; and
- researchers.

The product should remain understandable to casual users while preserving the evidence, attribution, privacy, and history needed for scientific reuse.

## The core user experience

A user can record a marine-life encounter with:

- date and time;
- location;
- description;
- habitat or depth where known;
- one or more photos; and
- an optional taxonomic identification.

Users are never required to guess a species. They may submit an unknown observation, identify only a broader group, or request help from the community.

## Observation and identification

TrackSea treats an Observation and an Identification as separate concepts.

An Observation records that an organism was encountered at a place and time.

An Identification records a user's proposal about what that organism is.

Community members may suggest taxa, explain evidence, agree with existing identifications, or propose alternatives. Expert verification remains distinct from community agreement.

This separation allows uncertainty, disagreement, corrections, and scientific history to remain visible and auditable.

## Personal value

TrackSea should feel useful even before a user thinks about scientific contribution.

The personal experience includes:

- a journal of encounters;
- maps and lists of observations;
- learning through community identification;
- progress toward a personal Marine Life list; and
- recognition of first confirmed discoveries.

The first version should emphasize discovery and learning rather than competitive gamification.

## Scientific and conservation value

Each sufficiently complete observation can contribute to a broader picture of:

- where marine taxa are observed;
- when they are observed;
- how their ranges may change;
- which habitats they occupy; and
- where identification uncertainty remains.

TrackSea should preserve provenance, evidence, confidence, attribution, and revision history so exported records are transparent and reusable.

## Privacy

Marine observations may reveal personal movement, fishing locations, sensitive habitats, or vulnerable species.

Exact coordinates and public coordinates must be treated separately. Privacy and authorization must be enforced by the backend, not only by the user interface.

## Interoperability

TrackSea should use a simple, user-friendly internal domain model.

Darwin Core compatibility belongs behind the scenes through a documented export adapter. Users should not need to understand biodiversity data standards to record an encounter.

## Product principles

TrackSea should:

1. create value for both the user and the scientific dataset;
2. preserve uncertainty instead of forcing guesses;
3. make scientific history attributable and auditable;
4. protect exact-location privacy;
5. prefer evidence over unsupported confidence;
6. remain simple and low-cost during the MVP; and
7. keep important decisions understandable to humans and AI coding agents.

## MVP focus

The MVP focuses on:

- accounts and user attribution;
- observations with photos and geolocation;
- taxonomic search at species and higher ranks;
- unknown observations;
- community identification and agreements;
- expert verification as a separate concept;
- a personal Marine Life list; and
- Darwin Core-compatible mapping.

## Future direction

The following may be explored after the core observation and identification workflows are proven:

- native mobile applications;
- offline capture and synchronization;
- AI-assisted photo identification;
- forums or richer social discussion;
- organization and researcher dashboards;
- gamification, points, levels, badges, and challenges; and
- more advanced reputation or expert-governance models.

Future gamification must never determine scientific authority.

## Product promise

TrackSea should make it easy for someone to say:

> I saw something in the sea, I recorded it honestly, I learned what it may be, and my observation can contribute to a larger understanding of marine life.
